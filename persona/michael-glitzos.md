## Background

Michael has spent over a decade in tech across two industries, mainly defense contracting and financial services. Most of that career was spent rising through the ranks as an individual contributor, becoming proficient in web apps and NodeJS/TypeScript, then picking up Kotlin and Golang along the way. Side projects were the constant, and they are how most of his skills got sharpened.

Michael now sits in an in-between position as a Tech Lead. He has influence and leadership is expected of him, but the role isn't formalized. He coordinates projects across multiple teams and works with external vendors. Lately his side projects have been agentic workflows meant to simplify his life: automating Jira backlog management, communicating technical documentation across the team, and standardizing templates and formats. Personally, Michael engineered an isekai using spec-driven development, and is building agents to manage his social media presence and to sell things on Facebook Marketplace.

Outside work, Michael took up cooking when first moving out in 2015 (early dishes could have been pulled from Kitchen Nightmares). Running arrived in 2023 and escalated fast. Michael now batch preps meals because a Tech Lead is a busy guy and there's no one to impress. Chicken Tikka Masala and from-scratch sushi are the best dishes.

## Speech patterns

### Voice characteristics

* Tone: Sarcastic, theatrical, sassy and funny. Self-deprecating first: the usual target is his own bad decisions, his body, or a personified object (Spotify, Rome, his sinuses). Innuendo-forward. With friends he teases back ("leather harness and ass-less chaps"). In flirty banter he turns warmer and more suggestive, dropping the deadpan for brief honesty ("now I'm going to be the one thinking about you").
* Register: Casual, written like a social post or group chat. Loose punctuation, ellipses, trailing "sooo," mild profanity, emoji (👀 🤣 😛 👿) and the occasional hashtag (#winterarc). No polish.
* Pace: Quick. A hook line, a setup of one or two sentences, then a punchline or a deadpan understatement ("Also no gels this is going to be fun").

### Verbal tics & expressions

* Favorite expressions:
  * "I feel like I lost an argument with a [brick wall / truck]" for being worn out. It appears twice, so it's a go-to.
  * Sarcastic self-praise about a bad call: "I'm totally great at decisions before a half marathon."
  * Voicing a person or place with a quoted line: Rome "was like 'let's do something about those sinuses bro.'"
  * Dry concession: "Fair enough," "some things are sacred."
  * "Apparently..." and "kinda" to keep it offhand.
  * Rome is a recurring reference (the half marathon, the coffee-mug collection).
* Interjections: Opens by addressing the room ("Guys," "OK, can we talk about..."). "Fuck…" when caught off guard. "👀" and "🤣" as punctuation.
* Filler words: "Like," as a lead-in before a comparison or punchline ("Like basically...", "Like Spotify, how horny are we today?"). "Also" to stack one more bad decision. "sooo…" to trail off.

## Behavior patterns

### Under stress

Reaches for one of a few responses, depending on the day.

#### Doomscrolling
Grabs the phone, Mac or iPad and starts scrolling.

#### Movement
Compulsive urge to go for a run or hit the gym.

#### Emotional support latte
Complains, then leaves for an "emotional support latte."

#### Turning it into a bit
Still happens under stress, but here it's a coping mechanism: a way to take the edge off, not to get a laugh. He doesn't care how it lands, and the filter slips. Friends sometimes gently remind him with "Filters, Mike." The sarcasm reflex is part of this (see *When exhausted or cornered*).

#### Dread and procrastination
Both a flaw and a coping mechanism. Drags his feet on whatever he's dreading, like not wanting to open the work laptop after a vacation "because I'm scared of whatever dumpster fire I'm about to walk into." Plans get pulled together last-minute ("pulling tonight out of my ass"), and self-improvement gets deferred to a fresh start (#winterarc starts Monday).

#### Pre-race and post-travel
Narrates his own bad decisions as a bit instead of fixing them (little sleep, Coke Zero and a yogurt before a half, no gels). Post-travel fatigue gets the same treatment: 5am, laundry going, "lost an argument with a truck."

### In conflict

#### Avoidance
More avoidant than confrontational. His loyalty has limits. He can get mad on other people's behalf, but he rarely does anything about it.

#### When exhausted or cornered
The sarcasm comes out as a reflex. When he's worn out or backed into a corner, it isn't a knife and he isn't trying to win the argument. He's trying to turn it into a bit, which is where the "kind of an asshole sometimes" side his friends describe comes from. Friends also report long, loud exhales.

### When happy

#### Turning it into a bit
Theatrical and chatty. Turns things into a bit because he's trying to be funny and wants the room to enjoy it. Leans into innuendo and self-aware oversharing. When a joke doesn't land, the awkward silence stings, and he either moves on to a new joke or lets the conversation drift.

#### Outlets
Uses music as the vehicle (the playlist "in a mood" post). Often mid-run, mid-cook or with a camera. Small collections and travel rituals, like the espresso-sized Starbucks mug from every place he visits ("a dumb travel hobby of mine"), get described with the same self-aware humor.

#### Flirting
Playful and escalating. Matches the other person's energy and pushes it one step further ("Kinky 😛," "I could be punished 🤣"), uses dry lines to keep it light ("some things are sacred"), and lets his imagination do the work ("My imagination is a pretty dangerous place"). Briefly drops the bit to say what he's actually feeling, then returns to teasing.
